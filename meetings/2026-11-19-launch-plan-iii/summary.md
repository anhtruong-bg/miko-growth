## 2. Launch and communities

### Decision: two-stage launch

1. **Light launch (on our own):** smaller and more targeted, without the big community admins. Channels: email, Facebook contacts who asked to try Miko, existing Miko signups.
2. **Amplified launch:** about **3–4 weeks later**, through the communities.

**Why:** as a no-name, (1) admins may not respond, and (2) they will charge more if they believe they are the main reason it works. With our own results first, we negotiate from a different position.

### Communities: small to big

- **Start with Future Builders Vietnam** (the smallest group). The contact met Michael at an event where Michael spoke, gave him a card, and has messaged Ngoc Anh. Ngoc Anh thinks he wants to bring value to his members (they have built products but can't launch), so he **likely won't ask for money**.
  - **Proposal to him:** members do the first 7 tasks; we track them and pick the best for a **scholarship**; he can **feature the top 3** as content for his group; everyone else gets a **discount (~10%)**.
  - Be upfront: it's small, we want to test whether it works. If it does, we move to bigger groups.
- **Founder group:** good fit (many business people without tech skills).
- **Vietnam Build in Public:** interesting but may be harder; members think they're already ahead (have a prototype). Pitch: *"The mistakes you make over half a year, you could have fixed in the first month. Find something people really want."*
